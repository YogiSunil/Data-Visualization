# Social Media Behaviors and Sleep Across Age Groups

## Question

**How do social-media behaviors and sleep patterns differ across age groups?**

## Visualization

An annotated heatmap compares the average for each measure across seven age groups. Rows are age groups; columns are social-media behaviors and sleep measures. Each cell displays its calculated average.

Behavior columns use blue: lighter means lower and darker means higher. Sleep columns use orange with the direction reversed: darker means lower or poorer sleep, and lighter means higher or better sleep. Each column has its own colour scale, so colour comparisons are within a column, not between different measures.

## Answer

In this dataset, the younger age groups report more social-media use and behaviors, while older groups report more sleep and better sleep quality. Average daily social-media hours fall from **7.44 hours** among ages 13-17 to **1.17 hours** among ages 60+. Average sleep rises from **5.42 hours** to **8.78 hours** over the same groups, and the sleep-quality score rises from **1.72** to **4.81**.

## Measures and Interpretation

- Social-media hours and sleep hours are averages in hours.
- Frequency responses are scored from 0 (Never) to 4 (Often/Daily/Always, according to the response scale). Purposeless scrolling uses the available answers: Never=0, Rarely=1, Sometimes=2, Yes=3.
- Sleep quality is scored from 1 (Very poor) to 5 (Very good).
- A higher behavior score means more frequent behavior. A higher sleep-quality score means better reported quality.
- The dataset contains 300 rows marked `synthetic_ai`; these results describe the synthetic dataset and should not be treated as evidence about the general population or as proof that social-media use causes sleep differences.
- Age-group sizes differ, so averages from smaller groups are less stable.
